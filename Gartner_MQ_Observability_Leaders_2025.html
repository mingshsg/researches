Gartner Magic Quadrant for Observability Platforms 2025

Leaders Quadrant
Strengths & Weaknesses

Comparative analysis of all 7 Gartner-recognized Leaders
Based on Gartner MQ 2025, Gartner Peer Insights, and independent analyst reviews — May 2026
✦ 7 Vendors · 12 Dimensions · 5-Star Ratings · GenAI AIOps Included
DT

Dynatrace

Davis AI causal engine · Grail data lakehouse · Smartscape topology · Highest Gartner Ability to Execute

◆ Strengths

AI-driven full-stack observability
  • Davis AI delivers causal-AI root cause analysis with automated remediation — best-in-class AIOps
  • Grail lakehouse unifies logs, metrics, traces, RUM, and business events in one queryable store
  • Smartscape auto-discovers full-stack topology in real time — zero manual config
  • Application security integrated — Runtime Vulnerability Analytics within the same platform
  • Full-stack coverage: infrastructure, APM, digital experience, business analytics

◆ Weaknesses

Where Dynatrace falls short
  • Davis AI alert fatigue — high false-positive rate in complex microservice topologies; teams report manually verifying most RCA findings
  • Premium pricing — among the most expensive platforms; DPS (Dynatrace Platform Subscription) consumption model can exceed $100K+/yr for mid-size environments
  • Proprietary lock-in — OneAgent and DQL create migration friction; full re-instrumentation required
  • Limited air-gapped / on-prem — cloud-first architecture challenges regulated environments
  • Steep learning curve for custom dashboards and DQL beyond built-in views
Gartner MQ 2025: Leader — #1 Ability to Execute · Best for: Causal-AIOps enterprises needing deterministic root cause at scale · SG SaaS: AWS · On-Prem: Deprecated
DD

Datadog

Largest install base · 900+ integrations · Watchdog AI · Broadest ecosystem of any Leader

◆ Strengths

Cloud-native breadth and developer velocity
  • Largest install base — 47K+ customers, dominant in cloud-native DevOps teams
  • 900+ turnkey integrations — the broadest ecosystem by a wide margin
  • Kubernetes monitoring is superb — auto-discovery of ephemeral pods and services
  • Watchdog AI provides automated anomaly detection with minimal configuration
  • Excellent developer UX — intuitive dashboards, Notebooks, and a powerful REST API

◆ Weaknesses

Where Datadog falls short
  • Unpredictable costs — per-host + per-metric + per-log pricing causes frequent bill shock
  • Custom metrics trap — Kubernetes labels auto-generate thousands of custom metrics, driving hidden spikes
  • SaaS-only — no on-premises deployment; strict data-sovereignty environments are locked out
  • Vendor lock-in — proprietary agent, query language, and dashboard format complicate migration
  • Log costs escalate fast — many teams disable or heavily sample logs to control spend
Gartner MQ 2025: Leader · Best for: Cloud-native DevOps teams wanting breadth, integrations, and fast time-to-dashboard · SG SaaS: None · On-Prem: None
SP

Splunk

Leading SIEM market share · SPL query language · Petabyte-scale analytics · Cisco-owned, separate O11y and Security products

◆ Strengths

Unrivaled log analytics at petabyte scale
  • SPL is the industry standard for large-scale log investigation — unmatched query power
  • Security + O11y brand portfolio — separate Observability Cloud and Enterprise Security products share the Splunk platform backplane, but separate licensing, deployment, and UIs
  • Observability Cloud unifies APM (SignalFx), infra, RUM, and log analytics
  • Petabyte-scale stream processing — purpose-built for massive ingestion demands
  • Cisco acquisition adds networking depth (ThousandEyes) and AppDynamics cross-sell potential

◆ Weaknesses

Where Splunk falls short
  • Extremely expensive at scale — per-GB ingestion pricing makes observability cost-prohibitive for high-volume telemetry
  • Fragmented portfolio — overlapping capabilities across Observability Cloud, Enterprise, and SignalFx
  • Cisco acquisition uncertainty — roadmap, licensing, and support quality concerns persist in the community
  • OTel instrumentation described by users as "complicated and oftentimes obsolete"
  • SPL learning curve is steep — powerful but not intuitive without dedicated Splunk admins
Gartner MQ 2025: Leader · Best for: Petabyte-scale log analytics with strong (but separate) SIEM portfolio · SG SaaS: AWS+GCP · On-Prem: Logs only
G

Grafana Labs

2nd consecutive Leader · LGTM stack (Loki · Grafana · Tempo · Mimir) · Open-source champion with 26K+ companies

◆ Strengths

Open-source flexibility and visualization leadership
  • Open-source DNA — LGTM stack provides a fully open alternative to proprietary SaaS lock-in
  • Unmatched visualization — Grafana dashboards are the de facto industry standard
  • Massive plugin ecosystem — endless data source connectors; vibrant community
  • Strongest OTel alignment — natively supports OpenTelemetry without proprietary collectors
  • Strong cost profile — open-source core is free; Cloud Pro adds enterprise features (SSO/RBAC/audit) at competitive pricing

◆ Weaknesses

Where Grafana Labs falls short
  • Weak AI/AIOps — no mature automated root cause analysis comparable to Davis AI or Watchdog
  • Fragmented backend — Loki, Tempo, Mimir are separate systems requiring individual tuning and expertise
  • Self-managed complexity — open-source stack demands significant in-house SRE expertise at scale
  • Limited APM depth — lacks code-level profiling and business-transaction tracing of Dynatrace/Datadog
  • Enterprise features lag — RBAC, audit logging, and reporting are less mature than commercial-first platforms
Gartner MQ 2025: Leader (2nd consecutive year) · Best for: Open-source-first teams prioritizing flexibility, community, and competitive TCO · SG SaaS: AWS+GCP · On-Prem: Full parity
EL

Elastic

OTel-native · AI Assistant + ES|QL · Agent Builder · Unified O11y + Security · Search-powered observability + GenAI-driven operations

◆ Strengths

Search-powered + GenAI-driven observability
  • Elastic Agent Builder — GenAI-powered integration builder; create custom agents and data pipelines from natural language
  • Elasticsearch-powered search — unmatched log correlation and ad-hoc investigation speed
  • OTel-native architecture — deeply committed to OpenTelemetry, reducing vendor lock-in
  • Unified O11y + SIEM — single platform for logs, metrics, traces, APM, and security under one license
  • AI Assistant with ES|QL — natural-language querying with automated insights and root cause suggestions
  • Maximum deployment flexibility — SaaS, on-prem, hybrid, air-gapped — strongest optionality of any Leader

◆ Weaknesses

Where Elastic falls short
  • APM depth trails Dynatrace/Datadog — code-level profiling and automatic service mapping are less mature
  • Kibana visualizations are improving but still less polished than Grafana for custom dashboards
  • Self-managed complexity — operating Elasticsearch clusters at scale requires significant tuning expertise
  • Market perception gap — still seen primarily as "log/search" rather than full observability in some segments
  • AI augments, doesn't auto-diagnose — Assistant accelerates investigation but doesn't auto-generate root cause like Davis; teams still drive the RCA workflow
Gartner MQ 2025: Leader · Also 2025 IDC MarketScape Leader · Best for: GenAI-driven operations + unified observability/security with full deployment choice · SG SaaS: AWS+Azure+GCP · On-Prem: Full parity
NR

New Relic

175K+ companies · 100 GB/mo free tier · Consumption pricing · Developer-first observability with the lowest barrier to entry

◆ Strengths

Accessible developer-first observability
  • Generous free tier — 100 GB/month ingest; lowest barrier to entry among all Leaders
  • Strong language agents — deep code-level insights for Java, .NET, Node, Python, Go, Ruby, PHP
  • Consumption-based pricing — pay for what you use; more predictable than host-based models
  • 175K+ active install base — massive community, robust documentation, and extensive learning resources
  • NRQL is approachable — lower learning curve than SPL or Elasticsearch DSL

◆ Weaknesses

Where New Relic falls short
  • AI/AIOps lags significantly — alerts fire but don't provide automated root cause analysis at Dynatrace/Datadog levels
  • Consumption surprises at scale — data ingest costs compound quickly for high-throughput environments
  • No integrated SIEM — zero security observability; teams must pair with separate security tools
  • UI refresh churn — frequent redesigns frustrate long-time users and disrupt established workflows
  • Enterprise governance — RBAC granularity and audit logging trail Splunk and Dynatrace
Gartner MQ 2025: Leader · Best for: Developer-first teams wanting quick time-to-value with low upfront commitment · SG SaaS: None · On-Prem: None
IB

IBM Instana

Zero-config auto-tracing · Sub-second granularity · Watsonx AI alignment · Microservices-first observability with hands-off instrumentation

◆ Strengths

Automatic instrumentation for microservices
  • Zero-config auto-tracing — discovers and instruments microservices without manual code changes
  • Sub-second granularity — captures every transaction at 1-second resolution; no sampled aggregates
  • Deep Kubernetes-native — auto-maps pods, services, deployments with real-time topology
  • IBM ecosystem integration — strong alignment with Watsonx AI, IBM Cloud, and enterprise middleware
  • Lightweight agent — lower overhead than Dynatrace OneAgent in resource-constrained environments

◆ Weaknesses

Where IBM Instana falls short
  • Narrow integration ecosystem — far fewer connectors than Datadog (900+) or Splunk for non-IBM environments
  • Limited log management — Instana focuses on metrics/traces; enterprises often need a separate log platform
  • IBM brand perception — viewed as "the IBM tool"; can deter non-IBM shops evaluating neutral platforms
  • Smaller community — fewer third-party plugins, extensions, and community resources than any other Leader
  • Dashboard customization is more limited than Grafana or Kibana — less flexibility for bespoke views
Gartner MQ 2025: Leader · Best for: Microservices-heavy enterprises in the IBM ecosystem seeking hands-off instrumentation · SG SaaS: IBM Cloud · On-Prem: Strong parity

Key Takeaways

01

AI Is the Biggest Differentiator

Dynatrace Davis leads with causal AI for automated RCA. Datadog Watchdog follows. The rest are catching up. If automated root cause analysis is critical, Dynatrace sets the benchmark.

02

Cost Is the #1 Buyer Complaint

Datadog, Splunk, and Dynatrace all face bill-shock criticism. Grafana, Elastic, and New Relic all offer meaningfully lower TCO — but even the "cheap" options require paid tiers for SSO, RBAC, and audit at enterprise scale. Nobody is truly cheap at scale — the question is predictability, not price.

03

OpenTelemetry Is Reshaping Lock-In

Elastic and Grafana lead OTel adoption. Splunk lags. OTel-native platforms reduce switching costs — a growing threat to proprietary-agent vendors.

04

Security + O11y Convergence Is Rising

Elastic uniquely delivers fully unified observability + SIEM in one platform (same license, UI, data store). Splunk has both but as separate products. Datadog Cloud SIEM is integrated. True convergence — not brand portfolio — is the growing buyer requirement.

05

Deployment Flexibility Unlocks Regulated Markets

Grafana and Elastic support on-prem, cloud, hybrid, and air-gapped. Datadog and New Relic are SaaS-only. Data-sovereignty requirements favor flexible deployment.

06

No Single "Best" Platform Exists

Every Leader owns a distinct niche: causal AIOps (Dynatrace), breadth (Datadog), security+scale (Splunk), openness (Grafana), GenAI ops + convergence (Elastic), accessibility (New Relic), auto-instrumentation (Instana). Match the platform to your architecture and budget — not the other way around.
